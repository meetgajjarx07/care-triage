# Safety questions: strontium, DEXA readings, lead notice, side effects

Source: algaecal.com FAQ, public filings, read Oct 2026. Demo knowledge base. Nothing here is medical advice and every reply should say to check with a doctor.

## Strontium
Strontium Boost is strontium citrate, not the prescription drug strontium ranelate that was linked to heart risk in Europe. AlgaeCal's position is that strontium citrate has a different safety record. Customers with heart disease, kidney disease or blood clotting history should talk to their doctor before taking it.

## Strontium and DEXA
Strontium is heavier than calcium, so once it is in the bone a DEXA scan reads higher than the real change in bone mineral. The company's FAQ says that if a scan shows a 10% increase, the strontium effect might account for a few percent of it. When a customer asks about this, say it plainly and point out that the guarantee still applies to the scan result as measured.

## Lead and heavy metals
Every batch is third party tested for heavy metals. In September 2026 a California Proposition 65 notice was filed relating to lead in a product. Prop 65 thresholds are far below federal limits and many whole food calcium supplements carry the notice. Customer Care should acknowledge the notice, point to the batch testing, and not argue with the customer.

## Common side effects
Digestive upset and constipation are the ones customers report most, usually from the magnesium or from taking all capsules at once. Splitting the dose and taking with food helps. Anything that feels serious: stop and see a doctor.

## Medication interactions people ask about
Levothyroxine (space four hours), bisphosphonates like Fosamax (customers often take both, ask them to involve their doctor), Prolia (same), blood thinners (vitamin K2 is in the product, so warfarin users must speak to their doctor first).

## How long until a scan shows change
The published studies and the guarantee both use six months as the minimum. Most follow up scans are 12 to 24 months apart because insurance pays for them on that schedule. Typical reported increases are 1% to 4% in the first six to twelve months. Individual results vary.
